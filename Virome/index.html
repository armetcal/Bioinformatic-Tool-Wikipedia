About

Viromics uses metagenomic sequencing data to identify viral sequences and characterize their diversity and function.

If run on typical metagenomic data (i.e. no virus-specific enrichment), then most of the viruses will be bacteriophages and other viruses that are enclosed by bacteria. Free virus particles are typically washed away during sample processing.

The following flowchart shows the overall workflow for viromics. The major tools required for each step are listed in parentheses.

Please note that this pipeline is a work in progress, especially as the field is actively evolving - please treat this as a guideline and check other up-to-date sources. Much of the pipeline is based upon two key publications:

1. The Gut Virome Database Reveals Age-Dependent Patterns of Virome Diversity in the Human Gut (Gregory et. al, 2020)
2. Diversity and ecological roles of hidden viral players in groundwater microbiomes (Pratama et. al, 2026)

Pipeline Steps

1. Read Cleaning

Clean the raw reads using FastP, remove human reads with Hostile, and check quality with FastQC/MultiQC. This is analogous to typical bacterial metagenomic pipelines.

2. Assembly

Assemble the cleaned reads into longer contigs using MetaSpades, assess their quality with Quast, and determine which contigs are circular with MUMmer. This is analogous to typical MAG assembly of bacterial genomes.

3. Identification of Viral Contigs

Identify viral contigs with DeepVirFinder and VirSorter2, then filter for quality. Validate ambiguous hits with CAT. Trim and filter with CheckV.

4. Clustering

Use MMSeqs to cluster contigs by genetic similarity. This is analogous to OTUs or ASVs from 16S sequencing. This step is important for statistics: fewer false positives (because of sheer number of unclustered contigs) and fewer false negatives (because individual contigs aren't abundant enough to reach significance by themselves).

CheckV is then used to check cluster quality.

Finally, clusters are separated by size, as small contigs need special processing.

5. Taxonomy & Function

These can be assessed in multiple ways. For large contigs, we use both VContact3 as well as the MGV database to assign taxonomy, and DRAM-v and MGV for functions.

6. Contig Abundance

The abundance of each clustered contig is calculated by searching for its sequence within the raw reads.

7. Viral Diversity

Viral diversity is assessed using MetaPop. Not only does this calculate traditional alpha and beta diversity metrics, like in bacterial analyses, but it also assesses microdiversity at the codon/nucleotide level.

8. Host Prediction

Most viruses detected using traditional metagenomic sequencng are bacteriophages. Most bacteriophages are specific to one bacterial host. We will use iPHoP, which aggregates the results of several tools to provide a composite host prediction.
